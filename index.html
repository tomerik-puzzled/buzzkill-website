The Concept

Friendship over fear.
Or the virus wins.

Ten years in the future, at a remote college house party, a group of friends chase a euphoric high with a risky new drug made from zombie saliva — only to discover the antidotes keeping them human have been compromised. Trapped and unraveling, they have to rely on each other, or risk turning before the virus does.

The Story

In 2035, stepsisters Joni and Samira, along with exchange student Ramon, head to a secluded country house for what promises to be an unforgettable party. The surprise comes when Rusty, a reckless outsider, arrives with a chained zombie — the guests plan to extract its saliva, a banned drug that delivers euphoric, transcendent highs.

Nine years after a contained zombie outbreak, an antidote exists, but users must inject it within hours of the high to avoid turning. Persuaded by Joni, Samira joins in as the group ties up the zombie and begins their trip. Each spirals into personal bliss or chaos, leaving Ramon — immune to the drug — to supervise. But when Betty, the host's cousin, fends off an assault and accidentally frees the zombie, the night descends into horror.

Continue reading

Joni and Samira return from their high to find their friends fighting for their lives. Several are bitten, accelerating their transformations. In the chaos, half the antidotes are destroyed, leaving too few to save everyone. Panic erupts as the survivors argue, manipulate, and betray one another to secure a dose. What begins as moral debate devolves into violent contests for survival.

Alliances crumble and the house becomes a slaughterhouse. As the zombies close in, Joni and Samira cling to each other, only to realize they've run out of time and antidote. Facing inevitable transformation, they seal themselves in a car to end their lives peacefully — until they hear banging from the trunk. It's Rusty, still alive, claiming to know where to find more antidotes.

The ending

Their last hope takes them to a suburban dealer's stash. The sisters inject themselves just as Rusty turns, slaughtering the dealer and unleashing the infection into the neighborhood. As the quiet streets erupt into chaos, Joni and Samira drive off into the night alive — having caused another devastating outbreak.

Vision & Visual Language

Buzzkill is a movie of two halves — the audience falls in love with these characters before the fractures appear, feeling both the intimacy of friendship and the cruelty of its collapse.

Day / Before

Hope, teenage thrills, and romance, with a dreamy nostalgic quality drawn from '90s indie dramedies. Pinks, golden sunlight, late-summer orange, and soft teal. The camera flirts with the audience on steadicam; cicadas and rustling trees fill the soundscape.

Night / After

Summer fades into night, and vibrant warmth gives way to cold blues and dark reds. Shadows grow harsh. The camera becomes a passive observer — locked off, or moving with tension-building dolly shots. Horror lurks just out of frame. Polarization and betrayal take center stage.

Why Invest

Contemporary horror continues to perform well at genre festivals and in the streaming marketplace. Buzzkill is built to hit the commercial sweet spot: a recognizable, highly marketable concept with a fresh comedic and satirical twist, produced on a contained-location, ensemble-cast budget that keeps downside limited.
